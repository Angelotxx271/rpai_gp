# Opportunity

## Pain point
Students work through the roughly two-month gap between TFG deliverables without knowing whether what they're doing still fulfils the requirements or scope of their topic, and only find out at the deliverable meeting. By then two months of work can be thrown away, and redoing it eats into the time for the next deliverable, which can stop them delivering on time. Drift can happen at other points too, but the gap between deliverables is where it hurts most.

## User, need, and insight
Final-year IE students doing their TFG, any degree, need to know, before each deliverable meeting, whether their work still fulfils the requirements or scope of their topic, through a failsafe that is not perfect but better than anything else they have, instead of finding out at the meeting two months later, because students will use AI no matter what, but they're more concerned with making the work look not-AI than with using it well, even as IE moves to "disclose it, no penalty".

## How might we
How might we help final-year IE students detect and avoid drift from their TFG's requirements and scope during the roughly two-month stretches of work between deliverable meetings?

## Open gaps
- Insight: how students use AI and hide it is a team hypothesis. So far it rests only on informal, second-hand reports (see interview-evidence.md), and it has not been tested
- User: "any degree" is a hypothesis. So far all the evidence comes from Computer Science, and AI habits (who pays for AI) may differ by degree. Not yet tested
