# Interview evidence

Real-user evidence gathered outside AI sessions. Each entry says who it came from and whether it is first-hand or second-hand.

## 01 · Participant 01, IE Computer Science graduate

- **Who:** 4th-year Computer Science student at IE who finished his TFG in 2026, so he is inside the target user group
- **How:** informal conversation with a team member, recorded 2026-09-21
- **Used in:** jtbd.md

### His own experience (first-hand)
- The TFG schedule was very stretched, with about two months between deliverables
- He relied a lot on AI
- He had a good relationship with his supervisor, which let him work in a better-guided way, so he was less affected than others
- He was still affected by the conditions at IE, one of them being the large gap between deadlines
- In his own words, what would have been better: the peace of mind of not finding out at the meeting that his work was faulty, and being able to focus on the next phase of the deliverable instead of redoing it, while also finishing other subjects' work rather than spending more time on the thesis

### What he reported about his classmates (second-hand)
- Almost no one finished properly on time
- In the two months between deliverables, people worked without realising they were doing something wrong
- They found out at the meeting, so two months of work was thrown away
- They had to use the next deliverable's time to redo the previous one, and many could not deliver the project on time

## 02 · Informal reports from other students (unnamed, second-hand)

- **How:** relayed by a team member; individual sources not recorded, 2026-09-21
- **Used in:** jtbd.md (Solution hired, Habit, Anxiety)

- Almost everyone relied heavily on AI to write the thesis. They had AI write it, checked whether it was right, then rewrote it so it wouldn't be flagged as AI writing, and hoped it would hold at the meeting
- Most Computer Science students pay for an AI subscription, some for more expensive plans. Most other students use free versions or the cheapest ones
- They keep using the AI they already pay for, or use for almost everything, because it's the best they know
- If an option offered to them compared fairly with the AI they can access elsewhere, it would feel like a no-brainer. This is a stated intention, not behaviour
- They worry whether a new option is good enough to switch to, because most products the university offers run old, outdated models

- **How:** conversation with a team member, recorded 2026-09-21

## 04 · 4th-year Computer Science student at IE

- **Who:** 4th-year Computer Science student at IE, interviewed by a team member
- **How:** interview conducted at university, recorded 2026-09-22
- **Used in:** persona-hypothesis-04.md

### What she said (first-hand)
- I asked whether she had ever needed a TFG document or guideline and not known where to find it, and how she resolved it
- She said she could find the thesis syllabus itself, but when she looked in the courses assigned for capstones, it wasn't there
- She stopped looking at that point, reasoning that if it wasn't in the intuitive place where it should be, it probably didn't exist
- She decided to ask her mentor about it at the next meeting instead of continuing to search

## 05 · Advisor A, TFG supervisor at IE

- **Who:** TFG supervisor and full-time professor at IE, supervising about 10 TFGs, no name
- **How:** in-person conversations with the advisor and with some of his TFG students, early October 2026; recorded 2026-10-11 by a team member
- **Consent:** the advisor had a general notion of what the conversation was for, but not its exact nature; whether the students were told is not recorded; no names or contact details stored
- **Used in:** personas/drafts/persona-hypothesis-06.md

### What he said (first-hand, relayed)
- He has limited time and would like to help his students more, but his schedule doesn't allow it

### What the advisor and some of his students told the team member (sources not separated)
- He supervises about 10 TFGs as a full-time professor, with maybe a few minutes per student and no time for long conversations or one-to-one meetings

### What some of his students said (relayed)
- It took almost two weeks from their first email to his first reply
- When they email him a draft or a change, the answer takes more than a week, sometimes longer, and comes back as a short email or comment rather than detailed guidance
- After about a month the cycle is the same: one short meeting so far and almost no guidance

### The team member's account of the students' situation
- They wait, stuck, and can't yet tell whether their work is on track. The team member's reading is that they lack experience writing a paper. Whether they are drifting is too early to see
- The team member's impression is that they are frustrated with the delays

### Stated intentions (not behaviour)
- He would like to be able to check how his students are doing without having to interact with them. This is the team member's account, which he confirmed as the advisor's stated opinion, not observed behaviour
