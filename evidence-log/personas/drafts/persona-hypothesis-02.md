# Persona hypothesis: Stall-on-doubt

**Status:** Provisional Persona hypothesis requiring real-user validation

## Shared Job to Be Done

When final-year IE students work on their TFG through two-month gaps between deliverable meetings without knowing whether they're on track, finding out at a meeting that the last two months of work were faulty prompts them to seek a way to go into each meeting already knowing their work meets what's required, so they can move on to the next phase.

## Behavioural archetype

A student who, when a doubt about their TFG work can't be resolved quickly, stalls on that part of the work and avoids it rather than pushing through the uncertainty.

## Decision-relevant profile

- **Relevant context:** Working on the TFG during the roughly two-month gaps between deliverable meetings, where advisor access is limited.
- **Most relevant current behaviour:** When a doubt arises and isn't promptly answered, the student feels anxious about whether they're on the wrong track, and either avoids returning to that part of the work or switches to other tasks instead of resolving the uncertainty.
- **Current approach or alternative:** Relies mainly on advisors and classmates to resolve doubts.
- **Decision-relevant constraints:** The advisor's (or another knowledgeable person's) availability is the main bottleneck; a secondary factor is finding someone who is and feels knowledgeable enough in the specific TFG topic.
- **Product or research decision affected:** 24/7 availability alone may not be sufficient — the team should research what makes an advisor (or an AI substitute) feel knowledgeable/credible enough for students to trust and act on, since perceived competence, not just access, may be needed to stop the stall-and-avoid behaviour.

## Evidence boundary

| Claim | Evidence status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Doubts accumulate and, when unresolved quickly, get dropped or ignored | HYPOTHESIS | General personal observation of group-project behaviour, proposed to transfer to TFG |
| Compulsively seeks out available people (advisor, classmates) to resolve doubts | HYPOTHESIS | Analogous school group-project episode (calling around teammates); not from TFG context |
| Stalls on / avoids the affected part of the work when a doubt goes unresolved | HYPOTHESIS | Student's own reasoning, refined during this exercise; no real-user TFG evidence |
| Advisor availability is limited during the two-month gap | SUPPORTED | Consistent with opportunity.md and jtbd.md team framing |
| Feeling the advisor/source is knowledgeable matters, not just availability | HYPOTHESIS | Student's own inference during this exercise |

## Validation gaps

- Whether real TFG students actually stall or avoid a part of their work because of unresolved doubts
- Whether the group-project doubt-clearing behaviour actually transfers to the TFG context, where advisor access — not teammates — is the main resource
- Whether "feeling the advisor is knowledgeable" materially affects whether students trust and use a resolution, versus availability alone being enough
