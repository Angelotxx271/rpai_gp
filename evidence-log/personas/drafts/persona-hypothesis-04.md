# Persona hypothesis: The Accidental Discoverer

**Status:** Provisional Persona hypothesis requiring real-user validation

## Shared Job to Be Done

When final-year IE students work on their TFG through two-month gaps between deliverable meetings without knowing whether they're on track, finding out at a meeting that the last two months of work were faulty prompts them to seek a way to go into each meeting already knowing their work meets what's required, so they can move on to the next phase.

## Behavioural archetype

A TFG student who doesn't know what information exists or where to find it, discovers critical gaps only through peer mentions rather than official channels, and avoids wasted work only by accident.

## Decision-relevant profile

- **Relevant context:** Starting work on TFG topic selection. TFG-related information is scattered across capstone course materials, workshop materials, emails, and unlisted locations. Some channels are lossy over time (Gmail-to-Outlook migration made some documents inaccessible).
- **Most relevant current behaviour:** When needing TFG guidance between mentor meetings: checks capstone workshop course materials first, then emails mentor if not found there. Waits days for mentor response. In the documented episode: searched obvious places (course materials, emails), didn't find the document, only discovered it existed and where it was located when a friend mentioned it.
- **Current approach or alternative:** Check capstone workshop materials → email mentor if needed → wait days for response. Alternatively: rely on peer knowledge when gaps are accidentally revealed through conversation.
- **Decision-relevant constraints:** Response time (mentor email responses can take days, creating waiting periods); information scatter (no single reliable source for TFG documentation); unknown unknowns (can't ask about documents they don't know exist).
- **Product or research decision affected:** Changes product scope from Q&A tool to: (1) Centralize all scattered TFG documentation (syllabus, guidelines, requirements) so students don't browse 20+ documents across courses and emails; (2) Make it queryable — students can ask without already knowing what exists; (3) Proactively flag deviations from guidelines rather than only responding when asked — because the core problem is not knowing to ask.

## Validation gaps

- **Most critical:** Whether this findability problem is common across TFG students or specific to this individual (degree, search habits, one-off bad luck). If most students find documents fine through official channels, this is an edge case, not a core persona.
- **Second:** What other students actually do when they need a document — whether they check courses then email (as predicted), ask friends immediately, or proceed without the document.
- **Validation action needed:** Talk to 2-3 other final-year students directly — have they needed a TFG document/guideline and not known where to find it? How did they resolve it?
