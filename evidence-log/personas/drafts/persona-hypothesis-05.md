# Persona hypothesis: The Unanchored Explorer

**Status:** Provisional Persona hypothesis requiring real-user validation

## Shared Job to Be Done

When final-year IE students work on their TFG through two-month gaps between deliverable meetings without knowing whether they're on track, finding out at a meeting that the last two months of work were faulty prompts them to seek a way to go into each meeting already knowing their work meets what's required, so they can move on to the next phase

## Behavioural archetype

A final-year IE student who works without a fixed, bounded research question. They dive straight into finding answers and sources before framing the problem, and — with no clear boundary to check against — keep bolting on interesting tangents. So they pull mismatched or unreliable sources, the thesis balloons past what the TFG requires, and they only realise deep into the work that it has sprawled or that the framing was wrong, and it has to be cut back or redone

## Decision-relevant profile

- **Relevant context:** Choosing and working a topic through the two-month gaps between meetings. Starts from a broad topic or a stance rather than a scoped, answerable research question, and never sets a clear boundary for what's in or out
- **Most relevant current behaviour:** Goes straight to hunting for solutions and sources without first defining the question or what would count as an answer — framing the topic as a position rather than a question (e.g. "a critique of 'tariffs' long-term benefits are worth short-term costs'"), grabbing whatever sources seem to fit including mismatched or unreliable ones, and reaching for elaborate answers over simple ones. With no boundary to hold to, they then keep adding interesting tangents, so the work expands past what's required and loses focus. The sprawl or the wrong framing only surfaces well into the work, often at or just before a supervisor meeting
- **Current approach or alternative:** Uses AI or web search to fetch answers and follow whatever threads look interesting, with no explicit step to define the problem, scope the question, or re-check the growing work against the requirements. Personal judgement and the next meeting are the only real check that the topic is sound and still in scope
- **Decision-relevant constraints:** No habit or scaffold for framing the problem and bounding it before and during the work; difficulty telling a reliable, on-question source from an unreliable or off-question one; interesting tangents are more tempting than staying inside scope; time pressure pushes toward the first plausible answer over the correct, bounded question
- **Product or research decision affected:** The tool cannot only check finished work near the deadline. It needs two intervention points: (1) at the start, make the student state the real research question, what would count as an answer, and the scope boundaries; (2) throughout the gap, flag when the sources they use don't match that question and when new work is drifting outside the stated scope. This catches drift at its earliest and cheapest point — the question itself — and keeps catching it as the work grows, instead of only at the meeting

## Validation gaps

- **Most important:** whether the two merged behaviours — chasing answers before framing the question, and letting scope creep — actually co-occur in the same student, or are really two separate personas that should be split
- Whether students would stop to define and re-bound the question if the tool pushed them, or route around it and keep wandering
- Whether "wrong data source" here is a framing problem (as claimed) or a findability/skills problem — keep this distinct from The Accidental Discoverer (ai-persona-03)
- Whether stance-shaped topics and tangent-sprawl are a real recurring pattern or just one or two cases
- Validation action: ask 2–3 final-year students across different degrees to reconstruct how they chose and scoped their TFG topic, which sources they used, and how the scope changed over the gap — did they set and hold a bounded question, or keep expanding?
