# Eliminate-Reduce-Raise-Create Grid

**Status:** Confirmed value innovation hypothesis requiring evidence

**Sources:** Run on 2026-10-11 with the course ERRC prompt (downloads/ai-prompt-ERRC.md). Baseline from market-research.md sections 2 to 4; context from opportunity.md, jtbd.md, assumptions.md, experiment-card.md and personas/ai-persona-01 to 04. #n refers to a row in market-sources.md; participant numbers refer to the Wizard-of-Oz results in experiment-card.md. The student chose all four moves and asked for hints and choices on the Raise and Create wording; most cell wording is AI-proposed options the student selected and confirmed (ai-usage-log.md, 2026-10-11). Revised on 2026-10-12 with the student's decisions on the falsifiers, the new-market intent, the non-customers, the Create move and the net effect for the student, and with evidence corrections from independent reviews (ai-usage-log.md, 2026-10-12)

**Target user:** Final-year IE students doing their TFG, any degree ("any degree" is a hypothesis; see opportunity.md)

**Job to Be Done:** When final-year IE students work on their TFG through two-month gaps between deliverable meetings without knowing whether they're on track, finding out at a meeting that the last two months of work were faulty prompts them to seek a way to go into each meeting already knowing their work meets what's required, so they can move on to the next phase

**Strongest current alternative:** The general AI the student already has on their own account (ChatGPT, or a paid Claude), plus their own judgement, with the supervisor meeting as the only authoritative check. The manual route (Blackboard or Campus Online, friends, the supervisor by email) is a close second

**Evidence basis for the baseline:** Chosen by the author on 2026-10-11 (market-research.md section 3). OBSERVED in the Wizard-of-Oz test: 3 of 10 used their own AI and 4 of 10 checked with an official source or a person (one 20-minute session, n=10). REPORTED by a team member about classmates in one technical school (unverified) and second-hand in interview-evidence.md 02. SUPPORTED: 89% of Spanish students who use AI use chat assistants (#166, not IE-specific). Limitation: a small sample and the author's judgement. The counts do not separate the student's own AI from the manual route (3 against 4), 2 of the 3 who opened their own AI had not started their TFG, and all of it was observed inside a session built around the simulated chat. Market sources show what exists, not what IE students choose

## Value innovation hypothesis

**Intent:** not to win the AI thesis-helper market against ChatGPT and Claude, but to open a different space: the **pre-meeting check**, between the supervisor (authoritative but slow: #16, #82; interview-evidence.md 05) and the student's own AI (fast, but unaware of the programme unless the student loads it in)

**Non-customers it aims to bring in (HYPOTHESIS):**
- Students who will not use AI for the pre-meeting check: a smaller group, but the team says it exists (nearest support, not TFG-specific: about 10% of Spanish bachelor students do not use AI, #38, and one BBA Final Project syllabus bans GenAI by default, #12)
- Students who, even with TFGPT, will keep using their own option as well

Compared with the student's own general AI, TFGPT is a checker, not a writer:

| | Student's own AI | TFGPT |
|---|---|---|
| Writing | Drafts freely (AI inference) | Suggests fixes to flagged passages only |
| Grading | Can estimate a grade if asked (AI inference; one tool sells this, #53, #189) | Never grades or gives an overall verdict |
| Programme knowledge | Only if the student uploads the guidelines (#20, #75) | Preloaded with the student's programme guidelines, rubric and dates |
| Context between sessions | Through projects or memory the student sets up (#20, #24), within plan limits (#18) | Keeps the student's topic and work |
| Who starts the check | Waits to be asked (scheduled tasks exist, #18; their use for this is untested) | Checks before each deliverable without being asked |

- **Net effect for the student:** no uploading guidelines, no re-briefing an AI, and one central place for everything TFG-related, plus a check that starts itself. In exchange there is no grade or verdict and no drafting inside TFGPT (both remain available in their own AI), and a second tool next to the first. Net effort falls only if a check in TFGPT takes less effort than their own AI with the guidelines uploaded (validation point 1)
- **Net effect for TFGPT:** lower: no grade predictor to build or calibrate (an avoided cost) and shorter generations than drafting (AI inference); higher: keeping every programme's guidelines current, storing students' work and running scheduled checks. Whether the total falls is untested
- **Positioning:** TFGPT is used instead of the student's AI of choice for the pre-meeting check, and lives alongside it for everything else

**Falsifiable hypothesis:** final-year IE students will use TFGPT instead of their AI of choice for the pre-meeting check, because a check that starts itself, already knows their programme's guidelines and keeps their context takes less effort than briefing their own AI, and IE will commit to its full maintenance and adoption. **We are wrong if:**
1. Students don't end up using TFGPT instead of their AI of choice for the pre-meeting check
2. IE does not commit to the full maintenance and adoption of the tool
3. TFGPT misses more than 40% of what a supervisor would otherwise flag as not ideal in the TFG
4. The check can be copy-pasted into a different LLM and it does the same, in which case TFGPT has no value

**How each would be measured (proposed by AI, confirmed by the student on 2026-10-12):**
- Falsifier 1: in real use over a deliverable rather than one session, against the bar the team committed for the Wizard-of-Oz test (at least 6 of 10 doing the check without opening their own AI for it, experiment-card.md). That bar measured three tasks in one 20-minute session, so reusing it is a proposal. Students in the second non-customer group who also run the check in their own AI count against this falsifier
- Falsifier 2: IE counts as committed only when it does all three: names an owner who keeps the guidelines current, gives access to them through Campus Online or Blackboard, and offers TFGPT to final-year students (defined by the team on 2026-10-12; validation point 2)
- Falsifier 3: the supervisor reviews the same piece of work, and TFGPT's flags are compared with the supervisor's (validation point 3)
- Falsifier 4: the same check task in a general LLM with the guidelines and work pasted in (validation point 1). "Does the same" means the same flags for the same effort: TFGPT is expected to give the same flags as a general LLM with less effort for the student, so we are wrong if the other LLM matches both. Which model TFGPT runs on is not decided. The effort claim in the hypothesis is tested here

This remains a hypothesis, not a validated blue ocean or confirmed business model

## Critical outstanding validation points

1. **Own AI against TFGPT (falsifier 4):** give students the same check task in a general LLM with the guidelines and work pasted in and in TFGPT with them preloaded, and compare the answers and the effort (Raise; the first gap in market-research.md section 8)
2. **IE's commitment and the guidelines (falsifier 2):** whether IE commits to maintaining and adopting TFGPT, and whether TFGPT can get current guidelines, rubrics and dates for each programme from Campus Online or Blackboard, and who keeps them up to date (Raise; also a precondition for Create)
3. **Miss rate of flags (falsifier 3):** whether TFGPT misses more than 40% of what the supervisor would otherwise flag as not ideal in the same work; wrong flags are recorded too, since they are Create's shifted cost (Create). First data point: a TFGPT stand-in flagged all 9 points one advisor raised on a team member's proposal (spikes.md, field comparison; n = 9 points, below the 15 needed for a result)
4. **Use instead of their AI for the check (falsifier 1):** whether students do the check in TFGPT rather than their AI of choice over a real deliverable, not only in one 20-minute session
5. **No verdict:** whether students who get cited flags with no verdict can tell if they're ready (Eliminate)
6. **Counts as feedback:** whether IE counts a tool that flags points and may suggest a fix for a flagged passage, with no grade and no drafting, as "feedback", and whether that makes IE more willing to back it (Reduce)
7. **Flags nobody asked for:** whether students read and act on flags TFGPT raises unprompted, or mute them (Create); this connects to the jtbd.md gap on whether students feel anything is wrong between meetings

## Evidence boundaries

**Based on real evidence**
- Wizard-of-Oz test (experiment-card.md: 10 final-year classmates across BCSAI, BBA, BAM and BDBA, one 20-minute session in a simulated chat): 3 of 10 opened their own AI; 4 of 10 checked with Blackboard, a friend, Google or the supervisor; participant 7 generated topic ideas in Claude and checked them in the chat; participant 3 got conflicting format advice from classmates. The copilot answered fit questions and every check was started by the student, so the 6 of 10 covers neither a no-verdict TFGPT nor flags it raises unprompted
- Sourced market facts (market-sources.md): TFG format and citation rules differ between the programmes compared (#104) and one programme's rubrics sit on Campus Online (#106); IE allows AI for feedback and requires disclosure (#8, #76, #78); Grammarly's grade estimate is explicitly not a grade (#189); in the BIE and International Relations theses the supervisor decides permission to present (#16, #82); AI that evaluates learning outcomes, including steering the learning process, is high-risk under the EU AI Act (#194; timing #195, #196)
- Late discovery at the meeting is REPORTED second-hand (interview-evidence.md 01)

**Technical spikes (the team's own work, feasibility only)**
- Field comparison (spikes.md): two team members, two advisors. Work checked with a TFGPT stand-in came back with 4 minor corrections; work checked in a plain Claude chat with part of the documents came back with 9, mostly major, and the stand-in then flagged all 9. n = 2, not blind, not a test of demand

**Based on Persona, team or AI inference**
- The value-curve scores the moves start from (for example programme-guideline knowledge at 1 for the student's own AI), the new tenth factor and TFGPT's target line were proposed by AI; the student accepted them on 2026-10-12. They are judgements, not measurements
- "Students can't ask about documents they don't know exist" rests on one first-hand episode, written up as ai-persona-03
- Eliminate, Reduce and the guideline part of Raise come from market-research.md sections 4 and 7. The Create idea of a check TFGPT starts itself comes from ai-persona-03's HYPOTHESIS ("proactively flag"), not from the market evidence. Most cell wording is AI-proposed options that the student selected and confirmed
- The cells for the student's own AI in the comparison table are AI inference except where a source is cited
- The EU AI Act effect is UNKNOWN in both directions: dropping grading may help, and unprompted flags may count as steering learning; legal advice is needed
- No row claims willingness to pay; whether students switch for the check is falsifier 1, still untested beyond one session

**Considered and not taken into the grid**
- An IE-rule-specific AI-use record (market-research.md section 7, implication 1): not taken into the grid; kept as an open option
- The supervisor view with the AI usage log (ai-persona-04): not in the grid; deferred, to be tested with advisors first

**Shifted costs (kept out of the grid cells by the student's choice, recorded here)**

| Row | Shifted cost |
|---|---|
| Eliminate | The student has to judge for themselves whether the flagged points add up to "ready" |
| Reduce | Drafting moves to the student's own AI, which may be paid (#24), with the effort of moving between tools |
| Raise | Someone has to obtain, load and update every programme's guidelines, rubrics and dates each year (#104, #106), with a dependency on IE for access; the student's topic and work are stored between sessions |
| Create | Unwanted or wrong flags could annoy students and push them back to their own AI |

**Contradictions and unresolved gaps**
- "Instead of their AI of choice" (assumptions.md, goals.md, experiment-card.md) and "alongside" (market-research.md section 7) are reconciled as: instead of their AI of choice for the check, alongside it for everything else (falsifier 1). assumptions.md and goals.md now note that scope; the riskiest-assumption and Sprint Goal sentences themselves are unchanged
- "Students will use AI no matter what" (opportunity.md need statement) and the first non-customer group (students who will not use AI for the check): the team holds both, the second as a smaller group
- Concealment: the opportunity insight (a team hypothesis) says students care most about making work look not-AI, while Raise and Create need TFGPT to store the student's work
- Participant 10 did all three check tasks in Claude: the negative case for doing the check in TFGPT instead of their own AI; the experiment card gives no drafting-related reason
- Meeting cadence differs by programme (#14, #15, #89, #96, #97, #105), which affects when "before each deliverable" falls. jtbd.md keeps "two-month gaps" in its statement and notes the difference in its validation gaps; opportunity.md qualifies it by programme
- Whether ChatGPT Edu is still live at IE is disputed (#4, #181, #188 against the team member's report)
- Willingness to pay, and who would pay, is untested; falsifier 2 makes IE's commitment a condition
