# Northfield School of Business, BSc Data and Business Analytics, Capstone Proposal Guidelines 2026-27

## Purpose and scope

These guidelines apply to every student who starts the Capstone in the 2026-27 academic year. The proposal is a short plan of the work you intend to carry out. It is not graded. It is a condition for being allocated a supervisor, and the Capstone Coordinator reads it to decide whether the plan can be completed in the time available. Each proposal is written by one student; pair and group proposals are not accepted. A proposal is checked against the rules below, and anything that does not fit them should be fixed before submission. The rules are the same for every pathway and every supervisor, and the dates given in this document are fixed for the whole cohort.

## Proposal template

A proposal has a title line and four parts, in this order. Each part sits under a heading that uses the part's exact title, as given in the subsections below. Every rule in the subsections is checkable from the text of the proposal.

### Part 1: Description of the intended work

Part 1 contains five labelled elements.

- **Problem.** One paragraph that states the business or analytical problem and says who is affected by it.
- **Research question.** Exactly one research question, written as a question that ends with a question mark. Sub-questions and second questions are not allowed.
- **Objectives.** Between 2 and 4 numbered objectives. Each objective starts with a verb and serves the research question directly.
- **Expected contribution.** One paragraph saying what the work will add for practice, for knowledge, or for both.
- **Pathway.** Exactly one project pathway, named as it appears in the list in the section Project pathways.

Everything in Part 1 must be about the same problem. Objectives that serve a different topic from the research question are not acceptable.

### Part 2: Methodological approach

Part 2 contains five labelled elements.

- **Design.** One or two sentences describing the overall approach, such as a comparative model study, a case study, a survey or an experiment.
- **Data.** The source, the period or size covered, and how you will get access. Data must be either public or covered by a written access agreement dated on or before the proposal deadline. Data that you do not have and have no written agreement to obtain is not acceptable, and an intention to ask later does not count.
- **Method.** The techniques you will use. Every technique must be tied to at least one objective from Part 1. Techniques that serve no objective should be removed.
- **Evaluation.** At least one measurable criterion, such as an error metric, a comparison against a named baseline, or a defined success threshold. A statement that the results will be "useful" or "sensible" is not measurable.
- **Feasibility.** Exactly one feasibility consideration: the main risk to finishing on time, and how you will handle it.

The design, data, method and evaluation must all address the research question from Part 1. A method that aims at a different problem is not acceptable.

### Part 3: Preliminary timeline

Part 3 is a list of dated entries. It must show all four fixed milestones, with exactly these dates:

- M1 Proposal submission: 9 October 2026
- M2 Data secured: 27 November 2026
- M3 Interim report: 12 February 2027
- M4 Final submission: 14 May 2027

You must add at least two activities of your own between the milestones. No activity or milestone may be dated after 14 May 2027, and the fixed dates cannot be moved. If your work involves human participants or personal data, the timeline must also include the ethics application, submitted no later than 4 December 2026.

### Part 4: References and critical literature summary

Part 4 has two elements.

- **Critical literature summary.** A short text that does more than describe the sources. It must name at least one gap or limitation in the existing work that your project responds to.
- **References.** A list of at least 8 references, of which at least 5 are peer-reviewed journal articles or conference papers. The rest may be reports, books or reputable websites.

Use the Northfield author-date style. In the text, cite as (Surname, Year), (Surname and Surname, Year), or (Surname et al., Year) for three or more authors. List the references in alphabetical order by the first author's surname under a heading called References. Numbered citations such as [1] are not accepted, and no other style may be used.

## Length limits

The proposal is limited to 450 words in total. The limit does not include the title line, the reference list or the tool use declaration. The Capstone Coordinator stops reading at word 450, so text beyond the limit is not considered.

Recommended lengths for each part:

- Part 1: 110 to 150 words
- Part 2: 140 to 180 words
- Part 3: 40 to 60 words
- Part 4 critical literature summary: 50 to 80 words

These are recommendations, not hard limits. The total of 450 words is the only hard limit.

## Project pathways

A proposal belongs to exactly one of five pathways, and the name written in Part 1 must match one of these exactly.

- **Predictive Modelling.** Building and testing models that forecast or classify outcomes.
- **Business Intelligence and Dashboards.** Designing metrics, data models and dashboards that support decisions.
- **Process and Operations Analytics.** Analysing flows, delays and costs in an operation to find improvements.
- **Customer and Market Analytics.** Studying customer behaviour, segments, pricing or demand.
- **Data Ethics and Governance Review.** Examining fairness, privacy, accountability or regulation in the use of data.

Pure software development and hardware projects are not offered. If your idea fits none of the five pathways, talk to the Capstone Coordinator before submitting. Do not invent a pathway name.

## Format and submission

Submit one PDF through the Northfield Learning Portal by 23:59 on 9 October 2026. Proposals are written in English. The title line must be at most 15 words and must describe the work. Do not add appendices. Use the four part titles as headings, in the order given. The proposal is submitted by the student alone.

### Declaration of tool use

The last line of the proposal must start with "Tool use declaration:" and say which generative AI or automated writing tools were used and for what, or state "none". A proposal without this line is incomplete.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need ethics approval?

Yes, if your work involves human participants (surveys, interviews, experiments) or personal data that can identify a person. Submit the ethics application to the School Ethics Panel no later than 4 December 2026, and show it in your timeline. Work that uses only public, aggregated or anonymous data does not need approval.

### Can I change my topic after submitting?

Yes, once, up to 27 November 2026. Send your supervisor a one-page change note and copy the Capstone Coordinator. After 27 November 2026 a change needs the Coordinator's written approval.

### Do I need to have my data before I submit?

You need either a public dataset or a written access agreement by the proposal deadline of 9 October 2026. The data must be fully in your hands by milestone M2, 27 November 2026.

### Can I submit late or ask for an extension?

A proposal received up to 16 October 2026 is accepted, but supervisor allocation moves to the next round. An extension of up to 7 days can be requested from the Programme Office before the deadline, with documents that show the circumstances.

### Does the reference list count towards the word limit?

No. The reference list, the title line and the tool use declaration are outside the 450-word limit.

### Can I write the proposal with another student?

No. Every proposal is individual, even if two students use the same pathway or similar data.

### Can I use a citation style other than the Northfield author-date style?

No. Proposals that use a different style, including numbered citations, are returned for correction.

## What supervisors expect

### What supervisors do

Your supervisor meets you every two weeks for 30 minutes, starting the week after allocation. Supervisors reply to written drafts within 10 working days. They comment on direction, method and evidence. They do not write or rewrite your text, and they do not provide data.

### What supervisors expect from you

Send a written agenda at least 48 hours before each meeting, and send any draft you want discussed at least 5 working days beforehand. Keep to the four fixed milestone dates. Tell your supervisor within 5 working days if your data source changes. Do the work yourself: a supervisor will not sign off a proposal.

### Who approves a proposal

Only the Capstone Coordinator approves a proposal. Approval is confirmed in writing by 23 October 2026. No tool, supervisor or peer can approve it on the Coordinator's behalf.
