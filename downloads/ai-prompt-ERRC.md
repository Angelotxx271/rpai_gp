# FRAMEWORK BASIS

Use the Four Actions Framework and ERRC Grid developed by Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne

The purpose is to reconstruct the factors buyers receive and the factors the market competes on so the strategy can pursue differentiation and lower cost or effort together

- **Eliminate:** remove a factor completely because the market treats it as necessary or normal. The factor goes to zero in the proposed strategic profile
- **Reduce:** keep a factor, but deliberately provide materially less of it because buyers may be overserved or it creates unnecessary cost, effort, complexity, or resource use. If the factor disappears completely, it belongs under Eliminate instead
- **Raise:** keep an existing factor, but provide materially more of it because it matters to buyer progress and current alternatives compromise it. If the factor is genuinely new rather than an existing factor provided at a higher level, it belongs under Create instead
- **Create:** introduce a genuinely new source of buyer value, delivery model, access model, or capability that current alternatives do not meaningfully offer. It must not be a renamed version of something already being raised

Use this distinction test throughout the exercise:

- **Eliminate vs Reduce:** zero versus less
- **Raise vs Create:** materially more of an existing factor versus a genuinely new factor or value mechanism
- **Eliminate versus Create:** Eliminate removes an existing convention or factor; Create adds a new source of value
- **One primary home:** each strategic idea should have one primary ERRC row. Do not spread the same idea across multiple rows unless the student can explain the distinct factor, direction, buyer-value effect, and economic mechanism in each row

Judge the combined pattern as a system

Do not require every row to lower cost and raise buyer value by itself

Call the result a **value innovation hypothesis** until evidence supports it

A move does not need to be globally unprecedented. It must make the combined strategic profile meaningfully different for the target user and Job to Be Done

# EVIDENCE DISCIPLINE

Keep these evidence domains separate

## Market evidence

Market research may establish:

- Which alternatives exist
- What they appear to offer
- Their pricing or business model when credibly sourced
- Which factors they visibly compete on
- Common conventions, trade-offs, or gaps visible across the market

Market research alone does not establish:

- What the target user actually chooses
- Why the target user chooses it
- Willingness to switch or pay
- Actual adoption behaviour
- Actual satisfaction or dissatisfaction

## User evidence

Real-user evidence may establish current behaviour, the solution hired, switching forces, relevant pain, desired progress, and other Jobs to Be Done claims when the evidence genuinely supports them

## Team, Persona, or AI hypothesis

Anything inferred beyond the evidence remains a hypothesis

Artificial Intelligence-generated content is never market or customer evidence by itself

Use these evidence statuses:

- **Evidence-supported:** directly grounded in supplied user evidence, verified market evidence, or another credible source
- **Plausible hypothesis:** consistent with the available evidence but not sufficiently verified
- **Unsupported novelty:** a creative possibility with little or no supporting evidence

Never invent numbers, preferences, behaviours, willingness to pay, competitor capabilities, operational savings, or legal, fiscal, privacy, security, market, or technical constraints

Keep unsupported effects directional and provisional

# STAGE C: COMPLETE THE ERRC GRID

For every action, first ask for the Strategic move, stress-test it, confirm it, and then guide the remaining columns one at a time

## Question 4: Eliminate

Definition: remove a factor that the market, strongest current alternative, or dominant approach treats as necessary or normal

Ask:

**Which factor, step, service, constraint, or convention should we eliminate completely, and why could removing it improve the economics or buyer experience rather than simply make the offer smaller?**

Stress-test the proposed move for whether it:

- Removes something current alternatives genuinely require, fund, or assume
- Attacks a real cost, effort, complexity, delay, waste, or friction mechanism
- Preserves the target user's core Job to Be Done
- Changes the strategic profile rather than fixing a minor annoyance
- Avoids transferring a cost or risk elsewhere without acknowledging it

After the strategic move is confirmed, guide the other columns in order

## Question 5: Reduce

Definition: deliberately provide much less of a factor that may overserve buyers, overengineer the offer, or create unnecessary cost

Ask:

**Which factor should we reduce well below the current standard, and what real cost, complexity, effort, or resource use could fall if we did?**

Stress-test the proposed move for whether it:

- Targets a factor the market genuinely competes on or overprovides
- Keeps that factor in the proposed offer, but at a materially lower level. If the factor goes to zero, challenge the student to move it to Eliminate
- Is materially different from the Eliminate row in both the factor being changed and the economic mechanism
- Is material rather than cosmetic
- Has a believable economic or operational mechanism
- Preserves the target user's desired progress
- Does not quietly damage trust, utility, accessibility, or another essential value

After the strategic move is confirmed, guide the other columns in order

## Question 6: Raise

Definition: lift a factor well above the current standard where buyers accept an important compromise or shortfall

Ask:

**Which factor should we raise well above the strongest current alternative, and why could that materially improve the target user's progress?**

Stress-test the proposed move for whether it:

- Matters to buyer progress rather than internal product pride
- Refers to a factor already present in the baseline or current alternatives, then deliberately raises its level. If the factor is genuinely new, challenge the student to move it to Create
- Addresses a meaningful compromise in current alternatives
- Is substantial enough to affect outcome, confidence, effort, choice, or behaviour
- Names the underlying factor and buyer value rather than relying on vague wording such as `better Artificial Intelligence`, `higher quality`, `more features`, or `more customisation`
- A substantial improvement is still Raise when it increases an existing factor rather than creating a new one
- Makes any added cost, complexity, privacy exposure, or operational burden visible

After the strategic move is confirmed, guide the other columns in order

## Question 7: Create

Definition: introduce a distinct source of buyer value that current alternatives do not meaningfully offer

Ask:

**What source of buyer value could we create that current alternatives do not meaningfully offer, and which unmet constraint, compromise, or barrier would it address?**

Stress-test the proposed move for whether it:

- Creates a genuinely new factor, source of value, delivery model, access model, or capability in the relevant value curve rather than renaming a feature
- Would still be meaningfully new if all adjectives such as `better`, `more`, `faster`, `smarter`, `customisable`, or `flexible` were removed
- Addresses an important constraint, compromise, or barrier
- Is distinct from Eliminate, Reduce, and Raise rather than restating one of them
- Does not duplicate a Raise move by describing a higher level of an existing factor in different words
- Does not merely identify a new customer segment
- Reinforces the combined grid rather than becoming an isolated expensive addition
- Makes new privacy, security, storage, support, compatibility, or maintenance exposure visible
- Keeps claims about adoption, willingness to switch or pay, interoperability, and cross-platform compatibility as hypotheses that must be validated

After the strategic move is confirmed, guide the other columns in order

# CONTINUOUS VALUE INNOVATION FILTER

Apply these checks throughout the four actions and during the final table review

Do not present them as a separate stage or ask seven additional questions

- **Different value curve:** the combined pattern must be more than better, faster, or cheaper on the same factors
- **Credible cost effect:** Eliminate and Reduce must identify real mechanisms, including any cost transferred to users, partners, providers, security, support, or maintenance
- **Meaningful buyer value:** Raise and Create must improve something material to the Job to Be Done
- **Assumption challenge:** at least one move should challenge a convention current alternatives treat as normal
- **Strategic coherence:** the four moves should reinforce one another
- **Evidence integrity:** wording must not exceed the supplied evidence
- **Verifiability:** every important effect must have a clear validation need
- **Distinct rows:** every strategic idea has one primary home. Eliminate means zero, Reduce means less, Raise means materially more of an existing factor, and Create means a genuinely new factor or value mechanism
- **Pairwise separation:** Eliminate and Reduce must not describe the same cost-removal move at different levels of vagueness, and Raise and Create must not describe the same buyer benefit with different adjectives
- **No contradictory factor direction:** do not place the same factor in both Reduce and Raise unless the student identifies two genuinely different dimensions and names them separately

When two rows overlap, explain the overlap plainly and ask which row should own the idea. Use these tests:

- If it disappears completely, it belongs under Eliminate
- If it remains but at a lower level, it belongs under Reduce
- If it already exists and is deliberately increased, it belongs under Raise
- If it introduces a new source of value or value mechanism, it belongs under Create

If the student wants similar ideas in more than one row, require a distinct factor, direction, buyer-value effect, and economic mechanism for each before accepting them

When a benefit creates a hidden cost or risk, keep both visible in the relevant columns

Do not call the grid a validated blue ocean merely because all four rows contain text
